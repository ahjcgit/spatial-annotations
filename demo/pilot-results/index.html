Resource measurements and their limits

These are sampled Android app and Python bench process peaks, not remote-model or whole-system memory. Sampling was about every 0.5 seconds; it cannot reliably resolve a roughly 0.2-second delivery phase. Zero CPU tick increments during a hold do not establish zero energy cost.

November 24 · Time for one label decision

Across 12 paired targets, a cropped view took a median 4.64 s and the full scene took 4.75 s from image preparation to decision receipt. With a five-second example deadline, crops returned 3 of 9 correct bottle labels in time. Full scenes returned 5 of 9.

The local image worker used a median 2.14 ms of CPU per crop and 3.62 ms per scene, with about 31 MiB resident at image-ready. The earlier physical run recorded a median 154 MiB sampled peak for the glasses app. The model ran remotely and its compute usage was unavailable.

          What to do next

          1. Test which details keep a label with its object

            In a November 24 replay of four photos, the reviewer identified 6 of 9 bottle patches alone and 9 of 9 with the surrounding scene. All three background patches stayed unlabeled in both reads. The next glasses session uses two bottles of the same model and an unregistered lookalike. Inspect the paired images and decisions · Run the glasses experiment

          2. Make placement useful, then measure it

            Use camera-space box overlays for the next software iteration. For see-through alignment, measure camera-to-eye mapping, per-eye offsets and distance-dependent error with the wearer. The current delivery path omits boxes; Android draws a list. Done when: static placement error and readability are recorded across positions and distances.

          3. Separate inference time from orchestration

            Run the same saved images through an explicitly timed local or authorized hosted endpoint. Record exact model/runtime, request/response times and usage. Add a freshness budget and movement invalidation before showing delayed labels. Done when: endpoint latency and stale-result rates are reproducible without a conversation in the timing loop.

          4. Test persistent identity under controlled conditions

            Collect separate reference/development/test images, independently label identities and boxes, and execute H0–H4 on the same held-out recordings. Use similar objects as planned, include swaps and occlusion, and keep failures. Done when: per-sequence wrong-object labels, correct coverage, recovery delay and reasoning cost can be compared.

          5. Close the display-lifecycle gap

            Repeat no-label trials with final status, pause/resume events and expiry acknowledgements saved. Capture per-trial wearer feedback. Power and thermal profiling should follow a stable display loop. Done when: commit, hold and clear outcomes are explicit, including interrupted trials.

          Research answer: this pilot demonstrates a real snapshot-to-device path. It does not yet show that visual reasoning improves persistent annotation correctness, or that its benefit outweighs its latency and cost.
